Phishing simulation

This was a practice email. Nothing bad happened.

Attackers send messages like this every day. Take two minutes to see the signs, so the real one doesn't get you.

The email you just opened

1From: DocuVault <notify@docuvault-share.example>

2Hello,

3Kara Lindqvist (a vendor contact) has shared Q4 Vendor Rates.pdf with you.

4For your security, this link expires in 24 hours.

5Open document  → hover shows docuvault-share.example/v/8KQ2-verify

  • 1
    The sender's address doesn't match the service.DocuVault's real address ends in docuvault.example. This one adds "-share" so it looks right at a glance.
    Check the full address, not just the name.
  • 2
    No name.A service you use would usually greet you by name.
    A generic greeting is one more reason to check.
  • 3
    A file you weren't expecting.Rate sheets and invoices are favorite bait because they look like normal work.
    If you didn't ask for it, check with the sender by phone or Teams first.
  • 4
    A countdown."Expires in 24 hours" is there to rush you.
    Slow down when a message tries to speed you up.
  • 5
    The button goes somewhere else.Hovering shows the look-alike address again, plus "verify" in the link.
    Hover before you click. On a phone, press and hold.

Where a real version of this click would take you

In a real attack, the link opens a page that says the document can't load until you "verify you're human." Here's what that page looks like:

The trick (picture only)
☑ Verify you are human
To view this document, complete these verification steps:
  1. Press Windows + R
  2. Press Ctrl + V
  3. Press Enter
  • 6
    A fake "verify you're human" step.Real checks only ask you to tick a box or pick pictures.
    Anything more than that is a warning sign.
  • 7
    Asked to paste and run something.This is the ClickFix trick. The page secretly copies a command. Windows + R opens the Run box, Ctrl + V pastes the command, and Enter runs it. You would install the attacker's software yourself.
    No real website or document ever needs you to do this. Stop and report it.

What to do next time

  1. Don't click. Use the Report Phish button in Outlook to send it to the security team.
  2. If a page ever asks you to press keys or paste something, close the tab.
  3. If you already pressed Enter on a step like this, call the IT Service Desk right away. Fast reporting makes it much easier to fix, and you won't be in trouble.
